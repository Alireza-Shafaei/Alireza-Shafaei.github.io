Where to split a deep network between a device and the cloud. What a wavelet decomposition leaks when an image is generated scale by scale. In progress Master's thesis2025 — present

LLM-Enabled In-Context Learning for Task Offloading in Edge Networks

Resource allocation offered as a service: a DNN-partitioning agent that uses retrieval-augmented prompting to choose where a network should be split between device and cloud. Queueing stability and Lyapunov drift constrain the decision, so latency stays bounded over time rather than merely looking small on average.

Early stage Research project2026 — present

Trustworthiness of Multiscale and Wavelet-Based Generative Models

A wavelet-factorised generator builds a coarse image first, then conditions each finer detail band on it. That factorisation is evaluated almost entirely on sample quality — and the trustworthiness literature for diffusion models has grown up alongside it without ever being made scale-resolved.

The question is whether scale changes the answers. Whether memorisation is scale-selective, with identity in the low frequencies and acquisition fingerprints in the fine bands. Whether a small coarse perturbation propagates into a large semantic shift downstream. And whether per-band privacy budgets and cross-scale consistency penalties can turn the decomposition into a defense surface rather than only a wider attack surface. Currently at the literature-assessment and threat-model design stage.

Supervised by Dr. Sajjad Amini
Wavelet diffusionMembership inferenceDifferential privacyAdversarial robustnessMemorization
In progress Research project2026 — present

Cost-Aware Retrain / Alert / Rollback for Production ML Under Concept Drift

Building the evaluation harness for a three-stage drift-response policy: a prequential loop, a cost ledger, retrain gates, canary deployment, and typed rollback. Everything is evaluated under realistic label delay, so the policy is judged on the information it would actually have had at decision time rather than on labels that arrive too late to be useful.

Supervised by Dr. Babak H. Khalaj and Dr. Hamid Hadibeik

Reinforcement Learning and Applications

Training Q-learning agents in continuous environments by reducing state-space resolution — extracting the states that carry decision-relevant information and discarding the rest, so a tabular method stays viable where it normally would not.
